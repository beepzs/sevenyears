Why the first seven years?

Because so much is set in motion before a child turns seven — the microbiome, the immune system, sleep, appetite and the brain’s wiring for focus and feelings. Parents deserve clear, honest explanations of all of it.

The First Seven Years began as a podcast and video series, hosted by Dr. Murray Clarke and co-host Bryce Wylde. Over more than a hundred conversations with pediatricians, researchers and practitioners, one thing kept coming up: parents didn’t want another hour-long episode at 11pm. They wanted the answer to their question.

So we built this library. Every conversation is broken into short, searchable answers, organised by topic and by your child’s age — with the full episode always one click away when you want the depth.

Editorial standards

How an answer gets from a conversation to this site.

  • Named, qualified voices

    Every answer names the expert speaking and links to their profile and credentials.

  • Reviewed before publishing

    Answers are reviewed before they go live. Nothing publishes as a draft.

  • Dated and re-checked

    Each page shows when it was published and when it was last reviewed. We revisit answers as evidence changes.

  • Education, not diagnosis

    We explain the why and share expert views. We never diagnose, and we always point you back to your own doctor.

  • Clear on uncertainty

    Where evidence is early or mixed — especially for newer therapies — we say so plainly.

  • Corrections welcome

    Spotted something out of date? Tell us and we’ll review it, and note the change on the page.

Relationships & disclosure

Trust is the whole point of this library, so here is how we handle money and relationships:

  • Our hosts. Dr. Murray Clarke has a commercial interest in the children’s nutrition sector. Where an answer touches a category in which he has an interest, the page says so.

  • Sponsors. Some episodes are supported by sponsors. Sponsor messages are always labelled, and sponsors have no input into which questions we answer or what the answers say.

  • Guests. Guests share their own clinical views and experience. Appearing on the show is not an endorsement of any product or service they offer.

  • No product recommendations in answers. Answers explain nutrients, foods and approaches — not brands.
